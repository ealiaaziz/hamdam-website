title: A suspicious email, or one you think may be a scam
symptoms:
  - suspicious email
  - phishing
  - scam email
  - fake invoice
  - someone pretending to be
  - clicked a link
  - entered my password
clarifying:
  - Did you click anything, open an attachment, or type your password anywhere?
  - Is the sender pretending to be someone inside the business?
---

Reporting one of these is never a waste of anybody's time, including when it
turns out to be genuine. The cost of checking is a few minutes; the cost of
not checking is the whole mailbox.

If you have not clicked anything:

1. Do not click, do not open the attachment, and do not reply, not even to
   ask whether it is real.
2. Forward it to this desk as an attachment rather than a normal forward
   where you can, because that keeps the original headers, which are where
   the answer usually is. In Outlook: New Email, then Attach Item, then
   Outlook Item.
3. Leave the message where it is for now rather than deleting it.

If you did click, or typed your password anywhere:

1. Change your password immediately, from a different device if you can.
2. Reply here straight away and say what you clicked and what you typed. Say
   it plainly; nobody is in trouble, and the first hour is the one that
   matters.
3. Do not wait to see whether anything happens.

Requests to change bank details, invoices you were not expecting, and anything
urgent that arrives from a manager and asks you to keep it quiet are the three
that get through most often. Urgency and secrecy together are the tell.
